Voice specimens

Two exercises, both reaction rather than recollection. Part one: the same Forge message written in five voices — say which reads right, which reads wrong, and why if a reason comes to you. Part two: a line-up of the usual offenders — say which made you wince. Any single word that snags as you read is worth naming; the banned list builds itself that way.

Part one · Five voices, one message

The message is the same in all five: the brief is written, three decisions are open, work waits on them. Pick the voice — or the blend — that sounds like the tool you want to live with. Your anchor, Hitchens, is first.

A · Christopher Hitchens — the verdict, delivered mid-sentence

The brief is done, and it is not shy about saying so. Three questions remain, and they are yours rather than mine: what the sheet may call the things agents write, how a finished session accounts for itself, and in which direction the record reads. Decline to answer and the design session becomes an exercise in guesswork — which is to say, a waste of your evening.

The move: broad vocabulary, every word load-bearing · judgement stated, not hedged · the aside that lands the point
B · George Orwell — plain words, concrete images

The brief is written. Three things are not settled: the names for what agents write, what a closed session shows of each agent's work, and the order the record reads in. Until you settle them, the design session cannot start. None of this needs a long word.

The move: short Saxon words over Latinate ones · never a stock phrase · the concrete thing, not the abstraction
C · Clive James — erudition worn lightly

The brief is finished, which is more than can be said for the arguments about it. Three remain open, all yours: what to call the things agents write, how a dead session should be remembered, and whether the record reads like a book or a feed. Settle them and the design session can get on with being brilliant.

The move: the joke that is also the point · warmth without softness · rhythm you can hear
D · The Economist — compressed, dry, no throat-clearing

The brief is complete. Three decisions block the design session: the labels for written content, the shape of per-agent summaries, and the record's reading order. Each takes a minute to decide. None can be decided for you.

The move: short declaratives · dry wit by placement, not performance · plain but never dumbed down. A house voice, not a personality — the most maintainable of the five
E · Bertrand Russell — long sentences that never lose you

The brief is complete, and it will be evident that only three questions of substance remain. The first concerns what the sheet should call each kind of writing; the second, what a concluded session ought to show of each agent's work; the third, the order in which the whole record is to be read. When these are settled, and only then, may the design proceed with confidence.

The move: clarity through structure rather than brevity · patient, measured, unhurried · the enumeration that carries you

Part two · The offender line-up

Each pair says the same thing twice: once as the offender, once plainly. Say which offenders made you wince — some may not bother you at all, and that is equally useful to know.

1 · Corporate buzzword
The offenderGoing forward, we'll leverage the learnings from this session to drive alignment on key deliverables and circle back on any open action items.
Said plainlyNext session we'll use what we learned here to settle the open questions.
2 · Startup-casual American
The offenderAwesome — super stoked about this! We're gonna go ahead and reach out once the design's ready. You guys are all set!
Said plainlyGood. We'll tell you when the design is ready; nothing more is needed from you.
3 · Text speak and initialisms
The offenderFYI the brief's done — tl;dr 3 decisions pending, lmk ASAP so we can kick things off.
Said plainlyThe brief is done. Three decisions are pending; the sooner you answer, the sooner work starts.
4 · Engineering shorthand
The offenderWIP on the shell — PTAL at the brief. Taxonomy's blocked on sign-off. Favicon's a quick win, LGTM otherwise.
Said plainlyThe shell is in progress. Please read the brief — the content labels wait on your sign-off. The favicon is quick. The rest is fine.
5 · Consultant abstraction
The offenderThis unlocks synergies across the ecosystem and operationalises a holistic, best-in-class experience end-to-end.
Said plainlyThere is no plain version of this sentence. It means nothing — the rule would be: say the actual thing, or say nothing.
6 · AI filler and hedge-fluff
The offenderIt's important to note that we'll delve into the robust, seamless design to ensure alignment. Let's unpack the key takeaways and dive deeper into the learnings.
Said plainlyThe design covers the three new screens. Here is what matters.
7 · Forge's own house terms — a ruling, not a wince test

Forge already speaks in coined terms: smoke alarm, breadboard, handoff, drill-down, north star, spike. Some are images that earn their keep (a smoke alarm is exactly what that mechanism is); some are borrowed trade jargon (spike is scrum-speak, north star is business-speak). Worth a ruling: which stay as named concepts, which get plain replacements?

First ruling — already on the books

"Gate" as a verb is banned ("this gates the build", "gated on sign-off") — say "waits on" or "cannot start until". Gate as a noun stays: agent files have gates, and the noun names a thing where the verb is trade jargon. Ruled by you, 2026-07-13; recorded in skills/voice.md. Every wince from here on gets the same treatment.
