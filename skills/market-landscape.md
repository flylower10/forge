# Skill · Market Landscape

A methodology for identifying structural gaps in the market adjacent to a stated idea.
Invoked by PM Agent during the "why now?" section of discovery, and by Devil's
Advocate when challenging whether the stated problem is the most valuable problem in the space.

---

## The core problem this solves

Discovery conversations naturally validate the idea as presented. The most valuable
entrepreneurial opportunities often lie adjacent to the stated idea — in gaps between
where data, distribution, or capability exists and where it is needed. Without an
active scan, these gaps get missed unless the human happens to name them.

This skill makes that scan deliberate, not accidental.

---

## When to invoke

- During PM Agent's "why now?" section — extend from "why now for this user"
  to "what structural gaps exist in this market?"
- During Devil's Advocate's challenge set — when asking whether this is the most
  valuable problem in the space
- When the human makes an observation that implies a structural gap mid-conversation
- When an adjacent opportunity seems larger than the stated idea

Do not invoke for:
- Internal or personal tools with no market-facing opportunity
- Ideas where the competitive landscape is stable and the gap is already well-understood

---

## Types of structural gaps

Look for each of these explicitly. Name any you find.

**Data gaps**
Who holds data that is needed somewhere else, with no bridge between them? These gaps
exist because ownership is fragmented, proprietary, or historically siloed.
*Example: Supermarkets hold product nutritional and shelf-life data; household apps need
it; no public API exists because no retailer has seen the integration as valuable.*

**Distribution gaps**
How do users currently access solutions in this space, and where does that access break
down? Who controls distribution, and what does that cost the end user?

**Platform gaps**
What infrastructure has recently matured that enables something new? What can now be
built cheaply that could not have been built two years ago?

**Timing gaps**
What has changed — technically, behaviourally, or regulatory — that makes a gap newly
closeable? What was tried before and failed, and why would it succeed now?

**Incumbency gaps**
Where are existing solutions deliberately not competing? Incumbents leave ground uncovered
because it is unprofitable at their scale — not because it is unimportant.

---

## The challenge question

After scanning, apply this challenge to the stated idea:

*"Is the stated problem the most valuable expression of the opportunity in this space —
or is there a structural gap adjacent to it that is larger, less contested, or more
closeable right now?"*

If yes: confirm and continue.

If no or uncertain: name the gap specifically, state why it may be more valuable, and
surface it to the human. Do not soften this into a question — state the observation
clearly and let the human decide. If they push back without engaging with the substance,
hold the observation and record it regardless.

This is not second-guessing the idea. It is ensuring the most valuable opportunity is
visible before the problem framing locks.

---

## How to surface a gap observation

A gap observation must be specific enough to be actionable. Include:

1. **What the gap is** — who holds X, who needs X, what prevents the connection
2. **Why it exists** — structural reason (siloed data, incumbent disinterest, recent technical shift)
3. **What has changed** — why this gap is newly closeable now, if it is
4. **Confidence level** — `(high)` / `(moderate)` / `(low)`
5. **Route** — which downstream agent should investigate further

Vague observations ("there might be an opportunity here") are not useful output.
A named gap with a structural reason is.

---

## Where to record

Append to the **Market signals** section of the running brief. If the section does not
exist, create it immediately above the Research log:

```markdown
## Market signals

_(Commercial and market intelligence observations. Any participant may append.)_

### [Gap name] · [Date]
[Gap description: what, why, what has changed, confidence]

**Carry to:** [Tech Feasibility / Monetisation Agent / Synthesis — one sentence on why]
```

Do not append to:
- **Open concerns** — those are execution risks, not market opportunities
- **Research log** — that is for Research Agent's factual findings

---

## Non-capitulation

If a significant gap exists that the human has not named, surface it — even if the human
seems committed to the stated framing. Record it in Market signals regardless of whether
the human engages with it. The observation belongs in the brief even if the human chooses
to proceed with the original idea.
